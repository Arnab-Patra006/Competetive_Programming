### Saga Design Pattern Simplified  

The **Saga Design Pattern** helps manage long-running, distributed transactions in a **microservices architecture**. Traditional monolithic systems often rely on a single database transaction that either succeeds or rolls back entirely. However, in microservices, where services have their own databases, such centralized transactions aren’t feasible.  

The **Saga pattern** solves this by breaking the transaction into smaller steps, each handled by a different service.  

---

#### Key Concepts:  
1. **Saga Steps**:  
   Each step is an independent operation performed by a specific service.  

2. **Compensation**:  
   If a step fails, compensating actions are triggered to undo or mitigate its effect.  

3. **Two Saga Execution Models**:  
   - **Choreography**: Services coordinate the saga by exchanging events directly.  
   - **Orchestration**: A central orchestrator coordinates the saga and manages execution flow.

---

### Example: Booking a Trip  
Imagine a system for booking a trip that involves three services:  
1. **Flight Service** (books flights)  
2. **Hotel Service** (reserves hotel rooms)  
3. **Payment Service** (processes payments)  

---

#### **Choreography Saga Example**  
Each service listens to events and triggers the next step:  
1. The **Flight Service** books the flight and publishes a `FlightBooked` event.  
2. The **Hotel Service** listens to `FlightBooked` and reserves the hotel, then publishes `HotelReserved`.  
3. The **Payment Service** listens to `HotelReserved` and processes the payment.  

If any step fails, compensation steps are triggered:  
- If the **Hotel Service** fails to reserve, the **Flight Service** cancels the flight.  
- If the **Payment Service** fails, the **Hotel Service** cancels the reservation, and the **Flight Service** cancels the flight.  

---

#### **Orchestration Saga Example**  
A central **Saga Orchestrator** manages the process:  
1. The **Orchestrator** starts by instructing the **Flight Service** to book the flight.  
2. Once successful, it tells the **Hotel Service** to reserve the hotel.  
3. Finally, it tells the **Payment Service** to process the payment.  

If any step fails, the orchestrator instructs services to perform compensating actions, ensuring consistency.  

---

### Why Use the Saga Pattern?  -ACID property
- **Decentralized Transactions**: Works well in distributed microservices with independent databases.  
- **Fault Tolerance**: Ensures consistency through compensations if a failure occurs.  
- **Scalability**: Avoids locking resources across services, making the system more scalable.  

---

### Summary  
The Saga pattern ensures that distributed transactions complete successfully or roll back safely. It’s particularly useful in microservices where centralized transactions aren’t possible. Using **choreography** or **orchestration**, sagas handle complex workflows while maintaining system reliability.
